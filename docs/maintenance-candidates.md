# For maintenance mechanics, millwrights and multicraft technicians

Ramsay's maintenance tests are written for people who keep plants running: industrial machinery mechanics, maintenance mechanics, millwrights and multicraft (multi-skilled) maintenance technicians. Most candidates already do this work or are close to it. This page is about what that means for how you prepare.

## Where the test sits in a typical process

Ramsay sells its tests to employers for hiring, promotion, pay-for-knowledge and training decisions. Its catalog lists all four of those uses. In practice that means you may meet a Ramsay test:

- **When applying** for a maintenance job, usually after an application screen and before or alongside an interview.
- **When moving up,** for example from trainee to journey level, or into a multicraft role.
- **Inside a pay-for-knowledge scheme,** where passing categories or levels can raise your rate.
- **As a diagnostic** for an existing team. Ramsay publishes separate diagnostic tests for this, with longer category breakdowns.

Ask the employer which test it is and what it is being used for. A hiring test and a training diagnostic are read very differently.

## Match your study to the job level

Ramsay sorts its tests by experience, using these definitions:

| Level | Ramsay's definition | Forms in these notes |
|---|---|---|
| Apprentice/Entry | 0 to 1 years of training or experience, needing 2 to 3 more years | Not covered here. Ramsay uses aptitude and basic skills tests at this level |
| Trainee | 2 to 3 years of training or experience, needing 1 to 2 more years | Mechanical Maintenance Trainee, MultiCraft Trainee |
| Journey | 2 to 5 years of training and experience | MecTest, MultiCrafTest |
| Above journey | 5+ years | C, B and A Mechanic Evaluations and similar |

If you are applying for an apprentice or production trainee role, you are more likely to see an aptitude test, which Ramsay describes as measuring the ability to learn rather than learned knowledge. These notes focus on the knowledge tests.

## Which O*NET jobs the tests map to

Ramsay links MecTest to the O*NET job title **Industrial Machinery Mechanics**, and MultiCrafTest to **Maintenance and Repair Workers, General**. If your job description reads like one of those, the matching test is likely.

## Habits that trip up experienced people

Experienced mechanics often score below what their skill deserves. The usual reasons:

- **"We do it differently here."** The test follows standard textbook practice, not your plant's habits. When in doubt, pick the textbook answer.
- **Rusty on the theory behind the job.** You may set relief valves every week without having done F = P x A on paper in years. Redo the sums on the section pages.
- **Avoiding the other trade.** On MultiCrafTest, the electrical categories are where mechanics lose most marks, and the mechanical ones are where electricians do. The published average on MultiCrafTest is about 29 out of 60, which shows how few people are strong on both.
- **Reading too fast.** Short questions invite quick answers. Words like "never", "first" and "most likely" decide the item.
- **Second-guessing the obvious.** Knowledge items usually have one clearly right answer. If you know it, take it and move on.

## Tips for union and plant candidates

- Ask your steward or HR contact whether the test is used for hiring, promotion or a pay step, and whether the results are reported by category.
- Ramsay's FAQ says it recommends local validation studies for employers with labour agreements. In unionised plants the test and cut score may have been agreed as part of that process, so ask how it is used.
- If you need extra time or another accommodation, ask when the test is scheduled. Ramsay's FAQ says requests should be made then.
