# How Ramsay scoring works

Short version: you get one mark per correct answer out of 60, the employer compares your score with its own cut score, and percentile charts in Ramsay's reports show how you did against other test takers. Ramsay does not publish a pass mark, and says the right cut score depends on the job and the local labour market.

## Raw score

Each form has 60 multiple-choice items, and your raw score is the number you got right. Ramsay's FAQ uses a 60-item example: 45 correct out of 60 is 75%.

Ramsay does not publish a penalty for wrong answers, so treat a blank as a wasted chance. If you are unsure, eliminate what you can and pick the best of the rest. Check the instructions on the day in case your employer says otherwise.

## Percentage versus percentile

Ramsay's FAQ draws a clear line between the two:

- **Percentage** is fixed. 45 out of 60 is always 75%.
- **Percentile rank** compares you with everyone else who has taken the same test. If you are at the 82nd percentile, you scored as well as or better than 82% of that group. It can drift over time as more people take the test.

Ramsay's technical manuals and online score reports include a cumulative percent and percentile chart, which employers use to judge where to set a cut score.

## Average scores Ramsay publishes

Ramsay's product pages show statistics from large groups of applicants and incumbents. These are the averages for the four forms covered here, all out of 60:

| Form | Group size | Mean score | Standard deviation | Reliability (KR-20) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MecTest, Form A1 | 11,030 | 38.40 | 9.08 | .87 |
| MultiCrafTest, Form A6 | 3,708 | 29.01 | 9.30 | .86 |
| MultiCraft Trainee, Form A3 | 3,654 | 33.40 | 7.93 | .82 |
| Mechanical Maintenance Trainee, Form A | 4,962 | 35.84 | 8.52 | .84 |

Ramsay notes that the Mechanical Maintenance Trainee figures come from the previous version of that test.

What this tells you:

- **An average MecTest score is about 38 out of 60, or 64%.** So a test where you feel you missed a third of the questions can still be an ordinary result.
- **MultiCrafTest is harder for most people.** Its average is about 29 out of 60, under half. Few candidates are equally strong on the mechanical and the electrical side.
- **Scores are spread out.** A standard deviation of about 9 means most candidates land within roughly 9 points either side of the mean.

## What counts as a good score

Ramsay's FAQ says it cannot recommend a specific cut score unless it runs a job analysis and validation study for the employer, and that a cut score "can vary depending upon the labor market and the job requirements". It suggests employers use the norm tables in the technical manual, or pilot the test with people already qualified for the job. Its catalog also describes optional "Job Profiles", sets of cut scores per category, which can be applied to reports.

So any single "pass mark" you see quoted online is guesswork. What you can do:

- **Aim clearly above the published average** for your form. As study guidance, not a Ramsay figure, scoring 45 or more out of 60 on well-made practice gives you a margin over the average on any of these forms.
- **Do not neglect your weaker side.** Some employers look at category scores against a job profile, not only the total. A gap in one category can show up even with a decent total.
- **Answer every item.** Unanswered items score nothing.

## What the employer may see

With Ramsay's scoring system, employers get standard reports, and with job profiles they can get a Group Gap Analysis report (a group's average raw score per section against the profile) and an Individual Graphical Report (one person against the profile's cut scores). Whether you see your own results is up to the employer.

## Retesting

Ramsay's FAQ says employment testing standards require employers to give people the opportunity to retest, and that employers should set written retest policies, ideally using an alternate, equivalent form. Ramsay sells alternate forms of both MecTest and MultiCrafTest. The waiting period is the employer's decision, so ask.
