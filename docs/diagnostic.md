# 15-minute diagnostic

Three original items from each content area, to show you where to start. Answer without looking anything up, then check your answers and read the table at the bottom.

- **Sitting a MultiCraft form?** Do all nine parts, 27 items, in 15 minutes.
- **Sitting MecTest or Mechanical Maintenance Trainee?** Do parts A to F only, 18 items, in 10 minutes. Parts G to I are electrical and are not on those forms.

These items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any Ramsay test.

If you already know your weak spots, skip this and take the [free Ramsay Maintenance practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/ramsay-maintenance?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=ramsay-maintenance) instead. A full timed run gives a better picture of pacing.

## Part A: Hydraulics and pneumatics

**D1.** In a hydraulic jack, a 50 lb push acts on a small piston of 1 sq in. The large piston has an area of 20 sq in. Ignoring friction, what force can the large piston lift?

- A. 50 lb
- B. 70 lb
- C. 1,000 lb
- D. 2,500 lb

**D2.** Apart from storing oil, what does a hydraulic reservoir do?

- A. Raises system pressure
- B. Lets air and dirt settle out and helps the oil cool
- C. Sets the cylinder speed
- D. Replaces the relief valve

**D3.** What is the main job of the receiver tank in a compressed air system?

- A. To store air and smooth out pressure swings
- B. To add oil to the air
- C. To raise air temperature
- D. To filter out water vapour completely

## Part I: AC/DC theory and power distribution (MultiCraft only)

**D25.** What current flows when 12 V is applied across a 4 Ω resistor?

- B. 3 A

- D. 48 A

- B. Ohm

- D. Farad

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, D1 to D27</summary>

**D1: C, 1,000 lb.** Pressure = 50 lb / 1 sq in = 50 psi. Force on the large piston = 50 x 20 = 1,000 lb.

**D2: B.** The reservoir gives air bubbles and dirt time to separate and lets heat escape through its walls. *Why not A:* pressure comes from resistance to pump flow, not from the tank.

**D3: A.** The receiver stores compressed air so the compressor does not cycle constantly, and evens out pressure swings. *Why not D:* some water settles in the receiver, but removing vapour completely is a dryer's job.

**D4: C.** GTAW (TIG) uses a tungsten electrode that is not consumed. *Why not B:* GMAW feeds a consumable wire.

**D5: B.** Heat can ignite vapour left in an "empty" container. It must be cleaned and confirmed free of flammable vapour before hot work. *Why not A:* an empty-looking drum can still hold explosive vapour.

**D6: B, about 707 lb.** Each leg carries 500 lb x 1.414 = 707 lb. *Why not A:* 500 lb ignores the sling angle.

**D7: A, 300 rpm.** 900 x 15 / 45 = 300 rpm. Small driving large slows down.

**D8: B.** A jaw coupling's elastomer insert flexes to take up small misalignment and damp shock. *Why not A:* a rigid coupling transfers any misalignment straight into the bearings.

**D9: B.** Oil thins as it heats, so viscosity falls.

**D10: A.** A centrifugal pump cannot pump air, so its casing and suction must be full of liquid before it starts.

**D11: C.** A check valve opens with forward flow and closes against reverse flow.

**D12: D.** Area depends on diameter squared, so half the diameter gives a quarter of the area, and the same flow must move 4 times as fast. *Why not C:* doubling would be right only if area scaled with diameter, not diameter squared.

**D13: C, 1.00.** Diameter is twice the radius: 2 x 0.50 = 1.00.

**D14: A.** The title block holds the part name, number, scale, material, tolerances note and revision.

**D15: C, 1.252.** The upper limit is 1.250 + 0.002 = 1.252. The lower limit is 1.250.

**D16: A.** Feeler gauges are thin blades of known thickness used to measure gaps such as valve clearances and coupling gaps.

**D17: B.** Pulling keeps you balanced and keeps your knuckles away from hard edges if the nut breaks loose suddenly. *Why not C:* striking a standard wrench can crack it. Use a striking wrench for that.

**D18: A.** Overgreasing makes the bearing churn the grease and heat up. It often settles once the excess purges.

**D19: C, 3,600 rpm.** 120 x 60 / 2 = 3,600 rpm.

**D20: B.** Overload relays trip on sustained overcurrent. *Why not D:* fuses and breakers handle short circuits.

**D21: A.** Swapping any two phases reverses the rotating field, so the motor reverses.

**D22: C, 6.** 0 x 8 + 1 x 4 + 1 x 2 + 0 x 1 = 6.

**D23: B.** The input LED shows that the module sees a signal on that terminal, which is useful for separating field wiring faults from program faults.

**D24: A.** Current must pass through the meter, so it goes in series.

**D25: B, 3 A.** I = V / R = 12 / 4 = 3 A.

**D26: A, 5 Ω.** Two equal resistors in parallel give half of one: 10 / 2 = 5 Ω.

**D27: C.** Power is measured in watts.

</details>
