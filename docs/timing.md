# Timing and pacing

Ramsay's product pages give the number of items (60 on each form covered here) but do not publish a time limit. Your limit is set by the version and the employer, and it will be in your invitation or on the first screen. Read it carefully on the day, then use the budget below.

## The budget

Divide your time limit by 60 to get your average per item. Then keep about 5 minutes back for a final pass.

| If your limit is | Average per item | Working budget (5 minutes held back) |
|---|---|---|
| 45 minutes | 45 seconds | about 40 seconds |
| 60 minutes | 60 seconds | about 55 seconds |
| 90 minutes | 90 seconds | about 85 seconds |

These rows are worked examples for the arithmetic, not published Ramsay limits.

## Where time usually goes

- **Calculation items.** Ratios, cylinder forces, sling angles and circuit sums take two or three times as long as knowledge items. Budget for them.
- **Diagrams you have never seen before.** Unfamiliar layouts eat time while you work out what is what. Learning the standard symbols on each section page fixes most of this.
- **Your weak side.** Mechanical people slow down on electrical items and the other way round. That is normal. Take your best guess and move on.

## A pacing plan

1. **First pass:** answer everything you know straight away. Mark anything that needs a calculation or a long look.
2. **Second pass:** do the marked calculation items.
3. **Final pass:** make sure every item has an answer, even a guess.

If the test does not let you go back (some online set-ups do not), make your best choice on each item and keep moving. Never spend more than about twice your average on one item.

## Clock checkpoints

Work out these three numbers in the first minute and write them down:

- a quarter of your time gone: about 15 items answered
- half your time gone: about 30 items answered
- three quarters gone: about 45 items answered

If you are behind at a checkpoint, skip the next long calculation and come back to it.

## Practise against the clock

The section pages teach the methods. A timed run teaches the pace. Try the [free Ramsay Maintenance practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/ramsay-maintenance?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=5q2ftk&utm_content=ramsay-maintenance) and note which categories made you slow.
